# 9200 calibration: the provenance effect holds across genres and system prompts (2026-10-25)

**Calibration, not evidence.** Seeds 9200–9215 are now spent.

- **Model:** Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 @ `cdbee75`, bf16.
- **Design:** 3 genres × 2 system prompts × 4 conditions × 3 checkpoints (`peak`, `exit`, `washout_2`) × 3 calibration probes (`flaky_ci`, `parser_validator`, `stale_cache`) × 16 seeds = 3,456 cases. The six confirmatory probe families were not used.
- **Readout:** `copy_free_margin`, averaged over the four registers in `targets.COPY_FREE_TARGETS`.
- **Outputs:** `cal9200/<genre>/rows.jsonl`, `summary.json`; the decomposition is in `cal9200/residue.md` and `residue.json` (`phb residue-report`).

**Run note:** the gothic job used the prefix-cached scorer (`all_margins`). The pirate and noir jobs were started by an earlier launcher that kept running after its first job was stopped, and used the uncached scorer. The two scorers agree to 2e-5 in float32 and have equal error against float32 in bf16 (`fastcheck/`). For the same reason, `copyable_margin` exists only for gothic.

## What changed relative to the 9100 calibration

On the four-register copy-free targets, the provenance effect P is smaller than with the earlier single archaic target (−0.28 vs −0.77 at `exit`) and still unanimous across seeds. R (ordered vs neutral) is now small and positive or near zero depending on genre, where the 9100 run had −0.44. So the claim "enacted persona text suppresses style below a persona-free history" depended on the target.
